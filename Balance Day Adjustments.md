## Definition
A **balance day adjustment (BDA)** is an accounting entry made at the end of an accounting period. It makes sure [[Income|income]] and [[Expense|expenses]] are recorded in the correct period, in line with the [[GENERALLY ACCEPTED ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES (GAAP)#Assumptions, Concepts & Principles|accrual basis assumption]].
- The end of the period is often 30 June, but it can be the end of a month if monthly reports are prepared for internal use.
- Some adjustments change the recorded values of [[Assets|assets]], [[Liabilities|liabilities]], income or expenses. Others record new information.
- BDAs are an essential part of accrual accounting. They are step 5 of the [[Accounting Cycle|accounting cycle]]: after posting to the [[General Ledger]], and before the [[Trial Balance|trial balance]].

## Why They Are Needed
BDAs make sure the financial statements show appropriate amounts at the end of the period, when reports are prepared.
- Adjusting **income and expenses** reports the right profit or loss (performance) in the [[Income Statement|income statement]].
- Adjusting **assets and liabilities** presents the right financial position in the [[Balance Sheet|balance sheet]].
- They usually arise from **timing differences**: between earning income and receiving the cash, or between incurring an expense and paying the cash.

If BDAs are not recorded, the income statement, balance sheet and [[Statement of Changes in Equity|statement of changes in equity]] will often be wrong. This affects their [[Report#Fundamental Qualitative Characteristics|relevance and faithful representation]].

## Types of Balance Day Adjustments
There are many BDAs. MPP1020 covers five:
1. [[Accrued Expenses|Accrued expenses]] (week 6): Dr Expense, Cr Expense Payable
2. [[Supplies|Supplies used]] (week 6): Dr Supplies Expense, Cr Supplies on Hand
3. [[Depreciation]] (week 6): Dr Depreciation Expense, Cr Accumulated Depreciation
4. [[Prepaid Expenses|Prepaid expenses]] (week 7): the entry depends on the recording method
	- Asset method: Dr Expense, Cr Prepaid Expense
	- Expense method: Dr Prepaid Expense, Cr Expense
5. [[Prepaid Income|Prepaid income]] (week 7): the entry depends on the recording method
	- Liability method: Dr Unearned Income, Cr Income
	- Income method: Dr Income, Cr Unearned Income

## If an Adjustment Is Not Recorded
The effect on the financial statements when an adjustment is missed at the end of the period:

| Adjustment missed                 | Income Statement                        | Statement of Changes in Equity | Balance Sheet                              |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| Accrued expenses                  | Expenses understated, profit overstated | Equity overstated              | Liabilities understated, equity overstated |
| Supplies used                     | Expenses understated, profit overstated | Equity overstated              | Assets overstated, equity overstated       |
| Depreciation                      | Expenses understated, profit overstated | Equity overstated              | Assets overstated, equity overstated       |
| Prepaid expense (asset method)    | Expenses understated, profit overstated | Equity overstated              | Assets overstated, equity overstated       |
| Prepaid expense (expense method)  | Expenses overstated, profit understated | Equity understated             | Assets understated, equity understated     |
| Prepaid income (liability method) | Income understated, profit understated  | Equity understated             | Liabilities overstated, equity understated |
| Prepaid income (income method)    | Income overstated, profit overstated    | Equity overstated              | Liabilities understated, equity overstated |

None of these adjustments involve cash, so the statement of cash flows is not affected.
