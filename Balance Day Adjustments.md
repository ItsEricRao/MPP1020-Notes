## Definition
A **balance day adjustment (BDA)** is an accounting entry made at the end of an accounting period. It makes sure [[Income|income]] and [[Expense|expenses]] are recorded in the correct period, in line with the [[GENERALLY ACCEPTED ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES (GAAP)#Assumptions, Concepts & Principles|accrual basis assumption]].
- The end of the period is often 30 June, but it can be the end of a month if monthly reports are prepared for internal use.
- Some adjustments change the recorded values of [[Assets|assets]], [[Liabilities|liabilities]], income or expenses. Others record new information.
- BDAs are an essential part of accrual accounting. They are step 5 of the [[Accounting Cycle|accounting cycle]]: after posting to the [[General Ledger]], and before the trial balance.

## Why They Are Needed
BDAs make sure the financial statements show appropriate amounts at the end of the period, when reports are prepared.
- Adjusting **income and expenses** reports the right profit or loss (performance) in the [[Income Statement|income statement]].
- Adjusting **assets and liabilities** presents the right financial position in the [[Balance Sheet|balance sheet]].
- They usually arise from **timing differences**: between earning income and receiving the cash, or between incurring an expense and paying the cash.

If BDAs are not recorded, the income statement, balance sheet and statement of changes in equity will often be wrong. This affects their [[Report#Fundamental Qualitative Characteristics|relevance and faithful representation]].

## Types of Balance Day Adjustments
There are many BDAs. MPP1020 covers five:
1. [[Accrued Expenses|Accrued expenses]] (week 6): Dr Expense, Cr Expense Payable
2. [[Supplies|Supplies used]] (week 6): Dr Supplies Expense, Cr Supplies on Hand
3. [[Depreciation]] (week 6): Dr Depreciation Expense, Cr Accumulated Depreciation
4. Prepaid expenses (week 7)
5. Prepaid income (week 7)

> [!todo]
> Prepaid expenses and prepaid income are covered in week 7.

## If an Adjustment Is Not Recorded
The effect on the four financial statements when a week 6 adjustment is missed at the end of the period:

| Statement | Accrued expenses | Supplies used | Depreciation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Income Statement | Expenses understated, profit overstated | Expenses understated, profit overstated | Expenses understated, profit overstated |
| Statement of Changes in Equity | Equity overstated | Equity overstated | Equity overstated |
| Balance Sheet | Liabilities understated, equity overstated | Assets overstated, equity overstated | Assets overstated, equity overstated |
| Statement of Cash Flows | No effect | No effect | No effect |

None of these adjustments involve cash, so the statement of cash flows is not affected.
