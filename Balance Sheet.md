## Definition
The **balance sheet**, also called the **statement of financial position**, gives a snapshot of an entity's financial position at a **specific point in time** ("as at …").

Compare the [[Income Statement|income statement]], which covers a period of time.

## Purpose
- To show the entity's assets, liabilities and equity.
- To give insight into the entity's financial stability and liquidity.

## Key Accounting Elements
- [[Assets]]: resources owned by the entity that provide future economic benefits.
	- **Current assets:** Cash, Accounts Receivable, [[Inventory]], [[Prepaid Expenses]]
	- **Non-current assets:** Property, Plant and Equipment (PP&E)
- [[Liabilities]]: obligations the entity owes to external parties.
	- **Current liabilities:** Accounts Payable, Short-Term Debt, [[Accrued Expenses]]
	- **Non-current liabilities:** Long-Term Debt
- [[Equity]]: the residual interest in the assets of the entity after deducting liabilities.
	- Components: Capital, Retained Earnings, Profit, Drawings

$$
\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}
$$

## Format and Presentation
There are two main formats:
1. **T-format:** assets on the left-hand side (LHS), liabilities and equity on the right-hand side (RHS). Often used for smaller entities.
2. **Narrative format:** assets, liabilities and equity are presented down the page.

- **Comparative information** (previous and current period side by side) lets users see how the entity's financial position has changed between the two periods.
- Generally, the smaller the entity, the simpler its financial statements look.
- In all cases, the balance sheet must **balance**: total assets = total liabilities and equity.

## Template (T-Format)
Include **only the asset and liability accounts** from the [[Trial Balance|trial balance]], along with the closing balance of capital from the [[Statement of Changes in Equity|statement of changes in equity]]. Always complete the report title and date.
