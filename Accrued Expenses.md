## Definition
**Accrued expenses** are expenses that have been incurred but not yet paid or recorded, for goods or services already received at the reporting date.

Common accrued expenses include interest, taxes and salaries (wages).

They are one of the [[Balance Day Adjustments|balance day adjustments]].

## Why the Adjustment Is Needed
The adjustment has two purposes:
1. To **record the obligations** ([[Liabilities|liabilities]]) that exist at the reporting date.
2. To **recognise the expenses** that belong to the current accounting period.

Under the [[GENERALLY ACCEPTED ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES (GAAP)#Assumptions, Concepts & Principles|accrual basis]], the expense is recorded in the period it is incurred, even though the cash is paid in a later period. Only the portion incurred up to the balance date is accrued.

## Adjusting Entry
The entry increases an [[Expense|expense]] (debit) and increases a liability (credit).

| Details/Accounts | Debit (Dr) | Credit (Cr) |
| --- | --: | --: |
| Expense *(expense increase)* | Amount | |
| &emsp;Expense Payable *(liability increase)* | | Amount |

Both accounts are named after the expense, e.g. Wages Expense and Wages Payable. The payable is a current liability.

## If Not Recorded
- **Income Statement:** expenses understated, so profit overstated.
- **Statement of Changes in Equity:** equity overstated.
- **Balance Sheet:** liabilities understated and equity overstated.
- **Statement of Cash Flows:** no effect.
