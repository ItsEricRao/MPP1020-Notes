## Definition
A trading business sells goods ([[Inventory|inventory]] or stock) to customers to earn revenue. Its success mainly depends on its ability to buy and sell goods at a profit.

Compare a [[Service Business|service business]], which provides intangible services rather than physical goods.

## Types of Trading Businesses
- **Manufacturers:** produce goods that are later sold.
- **Wholesalers:** buy goods from a manufacturer and sell them to a retail outlet.
- **Retailers:** buy goods from wholesalers and sell them to the public.

## Revenue Measurement Process
Profit (or loss) is measured by deducting the period's expenses from the period's revenue.
- **Revenue:** the primary source is the sale of inventory, called **sales revenue** (or **sales**).
- **Expenses** are divided into two categories:
	1. [[Cost of Sales|Cost of sales]]: the total cost of inventory sold during the period.
	2. **Operating expenses:** all other expenses.

$$
\text{Sales Revenue} - \text{Cost of Sales} = \text{Gross Profit}
$$
$$
\text{Gross Profit} - \text{Operating Expenses} = \text{Net Profit (Loss)}
$$

## Recording Transactions
A trading business records its inventory with the [[Perpetual Inventory System|perpetual inventory system]], the only method used in MPP1020. See [[Perpetual Inventory System#Double-Entry]] for the journal entries.
