## Definition
The **income statement**, also called the **profit and loss statement**, shows an entity's revenues and expenses over a specific period. The result is a net profit or loss.
- **Profit:** the amount by which income exceeds expenses for a reporting period.
- **Loss:** the amount by which expenses exceed income for a reporting period.

It covers a **period of time** ("for the year ended …"). The [[Balance Sheet|balance sheet]] is different: it is a snapshot at a point in time.

## Purpose
- To summarise the entity's financial performance over a period.
- To help stakeholders ([[User|users]]) assess the entity's profitability and operational efficiency.

## Key Accounting Elements
- [[Income]] (**revenues**): income earned from selling goods or services, e.g. Sales Revenue, Service Revenue.
- [[Expense]]: costs incurred to generate revenues, e.g. [[Cost of Sales|Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)]], Salaries Expense, Rent Expense, Utilities Expense.
- **Net profit (loss):** total revenues minus total expenses.

$$
\text{Profit (Loss)} = \text{Income} - \text{Expense}
$$
Only the income and expense accounts from the [[Trial Balance|trial balance]] go into the income statement. The profit (loss) is carried to the [[Statement of Changes in Equity|statement of changes in equity]], where it is added to (or, for a loss, subtracted from) opening capital. See [[Report#Link Between the Financial Statements]].

For a [[Trading Business|trading business]], expenses are split into [[Cost of Sales|cost of sales]] and operating expenses, so profit is measured in two steps (gross profit, then net profit). See [[Trading Business#Revenue Measurement Process]].

- Individual items go in the inner $ column. Subtotals and totals go in the outer $ column, e.g. total expenses on the same line as the last expense.
- Gross profit is net sales less COGS and inventory loss. Other income is then added, and total expenses are deducted, to give net profit.
