## Definition
A **transaction** is an external exchange of something of value between two or more entities. **Events** include increases in asset prices during an accounting period, and allocating the cost of long-lived assets to different accounting periods.

A **business transaction** involves a flow of resources into, within or out of the business. To be a business transaction, the transaction must:
- Be related to the business (the business must be one of the parties)
- Involve two or more parties

Business transactions and events must be recorded because they affect assets, liabilities and equity. Under the [[GENERALLY ACCEPTED ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES (GAAP)#Assumptions, Concepts & Principles|accounting entity concept]], always interpret a transaction from the business's point of view: the business must give or receive something, otherwise there is no transaction to record.

A transaction is either on a **cash basis** or an **accrual (credit) basis**.

## Cash Basis
A **cash basis** transaction involves an immediate exchange of money.

**Examples**
- A business purchases a new computer for $1,200 and pays in cash.
- A business provides a service and receives $500 cash from the customer.

## Accrual Basis (Credit)
An **accrual basis (credit)** transaction is a business event that needs recording but does not affect the business bank account at that time. Cash is neither received nor paid yet, but there will be a cash flow (an inflow or an outflow) at a later time.

**Examples**
- A business purchases office stationery on credit for $300 from a supplier.
- A business invoices a customer for a $400 service provided.

### Credit Terms
**Credit terms** are the conditions under which a seller is willing to allow the buyer to purchase goods or services on credit. These terms specify:
- Payment due date
- Discounts for early payment
- Interest or penalties for late payment

## Analysis
**Transaction analysis** is the process of identifying the specific effects of transactions and events on the [[Accounting#Accounting Equation|accounting equation]]. The equation must **ALWAYS balance**.

Steps:
- Read the transaction
- Identify the nature of the transaction (business or not)
- Identify the elements affected by this transaction.
- Complete the accounting equation and check the equation balances.

Once analysed, the transaction is recorded in the [[General Journal]] using the [[Debit and Credit|rules of debit and credit]].
