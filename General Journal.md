## Definition
A **journal** is a historical record of business [[Transaction|transactions]] or events. The **General Journal (GJ)** is the primary accounting book where business transactions are first recorded.
- Transactions are recorded in **chronological order**: by date, from earliest to latest, as they happen.
- It follows the double-entry system (debits = credits). See [[Debit and Credit]].

## Why Use a Journal
- It shows the complete effect of a transaction in one place.
- It gives a chronological record of transactions that cross-references both the source document and the ledger entry.
- It helps prevent or locate errors, because the debit and credit amounts for each entry can be compared easily.

## Format of a Journal Entry
Each journal entry must include:
1. **Date:** the date of the transaction.
2. **Accounts affected:** the accounts debited are written first, then the accounts credited (indented).
3. **Debit and credit amounts:** recorded in the debit and credit columns.
4. **Narration:** a brief explanation of the transaction.

## Steps for Journal Entries
1. Find out which accounts are affected by the transaction.
2. Classify each account as [[Assets|A]], [[Liabilities|L]], [[Equity|OE]], [[Income]] or [[Expense]].
3. State whether each account is increased or decreased.
4. Use the [[Debit and Credit#Rules of Debit and Credit|rules of debit and credit]] to decide whether each account gets a debit or a credit entry.
5. Record a narration.

Each entry is then posted to the [[General Ledger]].
